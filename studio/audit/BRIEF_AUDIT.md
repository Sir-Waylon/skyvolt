## Your job, in this order
1. FACTUALITY: check every question and every answer/option/explanation. Compute every numeric answer yourself (use python to check). Check units, rounding, tolerances (num.tol is a relative fraction, e.g. 0.02 = 2%), sig figs, and that exactly one option is correct. Catch ambiguity (two defensible answers), outdated or regional claims, wrong thresholds (e.g. EF scale wind ranges, SPC risk categories, METAR coding, color codes, logic gate truth tables, LED forward voltages, KVL signs). Fix or delete anything wrong.
2. DIFFICULTY and COVERAGE: the topic needs a wide range: about 15% easy (l=1, b -1), 35% core (l=2, b 0), 35% challenge (l=3, b 0.8 to 1.3), 15% very hard competition style (l=3, b 1.5 to 2.2). Existing questions often skew to "core". Re-level questions whose level is wrong (patch l and b). Add questions where a skill, subtopic or rule-scope item is missing or thin. Hard means multi-step reasoning, data/figure interpretation, applying a concept to a new scenario, and spotting misconceptions, NOT obscure trivia or longer wording.
3. LEARNING SCIENCE (cite none in the question text; this is design guidance): prefer questions that require retrieval (free recall or numeric entry) over recognition; include "why/how" questions (elaborative interrogation), prediction before explanation, transfer to a new context, and contrasting cases of commonly confused ideas (e.g. voltage vs current, watch vs warning, mesocyclone vs funnel). Distractors must be plausible misconceptions, never silly. Stems short, concrete, one idea, no giveaways, no "obviously/simply", no "all/none of the above", no negatives unless bold-worthy. Every question needs `w` (one-line why, plain words) and `sol` (2 to 4 short steps that TEACH the reasoning and name the misconception). Keep reading level around grade 8; keep technical terms but define on first use in the explanation.
4. ENGAGEMENT: scenario hooks help (a storm chaser deciding, a robot's circuit, a tournament-style station), but keep stems under ~40 words. Vary question types (mcq, numeric entry, short text, order, match, multi-select). Avoid near-duplicates: merge or delete repeats and lookalike templates that teach nothing new.
5. WRITE NEW QUESTIONS: add the number requested in your assignment. They must be ORIGINAL (not copied from tests or books), factually verified, and spread across the difficulty range with extra weight on hard (about 50% level 3 with b 0.8 to 2.2). Mix in "lock it in" items: single core facts every competitor must know cold (l=1 or 2, b -0.5..0) with an unambiguous answer.
