## LESSON opt-L1: Light travels in straight lines · Reflection
KEY opt-L1#0 | Light travels in straight lines | Light moves in straight rays at about 3 × 10⁸ m/s. We see an object when its light reaches our eyes. Shadows form where light is blocked. | CHECK Q: Light travels at 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum. A material has n = 1.5. What is the light speed in it, in 10⁸ m/s? (3 sig figs)
KEY opt-L1#1 | Reflection | A ray hits a mirror and bounces off. The angle in equals the angle out, both measured from the normal (the line at 90° to the surface). | FORMULA: θᵢ = θᵣ | CHECK Q: A light ray hits a flat mirror at 63° from the normal. What is the angle of reflection?
KEY opt-L1#2 | Refraction | Light bends when it passes between materials because it changes speed. The index of refraction is n = c/v. Snell's law links the angles. | FORMULA: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ | CHECK Q: Light travels at 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum. A material has n = 1.52. What is the light speed in it, in 10⁸ m/s? (3 sig figs)
## LESSON opt-L2: Total internal reflection · Lenses and mirrors
KEY opt-L2#0 | Total internal reflection | Going from a slow material to a faster one, the ray bends away from the normal. Past the critical angle sin θc = n₂/n₁ all the light reflects. Optical fibres use this. | FORMULA: sin θc = n₂/n₁ | CHECK Q: A light ray hits a flat mirror at 47° from the normal. What is the angle of reflection?
KEY opt-L2#1 | Lenses and mirrors | A converging lens or concave mirror bends rays to a focus at distance f. The thin-lens equation connects object distance d₀, image distance dᵢ and focal length f. | FORMULA: 1/f = 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ | CHECK Q: A converging lens has focal length 21 cm. An object is 27 cm away. How far from the lens is the image? (cm, 1 decimal)
