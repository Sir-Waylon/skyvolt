## LESSON st-L1: Centre and spread · The normal distribution
KEY st-L1#0 | Centre and spread | The mean is the balance point; the median is the middle value and resists outliers. Standard deviation s measures typical distance from the mean: s = √(Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1)). | FORMULA: s = √(Σ(x − x̄)²/(n−1)) | CHECK Q: Find the median of 26, 9, 8, 23, 10.
KEY st-L1#1 | The normal distribution | A bell curve described by mean μ and standard deviation σ. A z-score counts standard deviations from the mean: z = (x − μ)/σ. About 68%, 95% and 99.7% lie within 1, 2 and 3 σ. | FORMULA: z = (x − μ)/σ | CHECK Q: Scores have mean 73 and standard deviation 12. Find the z-score of 85.
KEY st-L1#2 | Sampling and the CLT | Averages of many random samples pile up in a bell shape around the true mean, with standard error σ/√n. Bigger samples give narrower curves. | FORMULA: SE = σ/√n | CHECK Q: A population has σ = 18. Find the standard error of the mean for samples of size 25 (2 decimals).
## LESSON st-L2: Confidence intervals · Hypothesis tests
KEY st-L2#0 | Confidence intervals | An interval estimate: x̄ ± z*·σ/√n. A 95% interval uses z* ≈ 1.96. The 95% describes the method, not one interval. | FORMULA: x̄ ± z*·σ/√n | CHECK Q: x̄ = 66, σ = 9, n = 49. Find the margin of error of a 95% confidence interval (z* = 1.96, 2 decimals).
KEY st-L2#1 | Hypothesis tests | Assume the null hypothesis, then ask how surprising your data would be. The test statistic z = (x̄ − μ₀)/(σ/√n) measures that. A small p-value means the data would be rare if the null were true. | FORMULA: z = (x̄ − μ₀)/(σ/√n)
