# circuit-lab-b / diode: Diodes & LEDs  (104 questions, 31 families)
## STUDY NOTE (what students are taught)
Silicon PN junction: P-type (holes) joined to N-type (electrons) forms a depletion region with a barrier of about 0.7 V. Forward bias (anode positive) narrows it; reverse bias widens it. Forward-biased silicon diode conducts with about 0.7 V drop. Reverse biased: acts like an open switch (tiny leakage) until breakdown. LED: a diode that emits light. Forward drop about 1.8 to 2.2 V red/yellow/green, about 3 V blue/white. Longer lead = anode; flat side = cathode. LED resistor: R = (V_supply − V_f)/I. Round up to a standard value, never down. P = I²R (check the ¼ W rating). Series LEDs: same current; drops add; the supply must exceed the sum. Parallel LEDs: give each its own resistor. Diode drops add in series: two silicon diodes = 1.4 V. Uses: rectifier (AC to DC), reverse-polarity protection (series diode, costs 0.7 V), flyback protection across relay coils, Zener (reverse breakdown for a fixed voltage), photodiode and solar cell (light makes current). Worked example. 9.0 V, LED 2.1 V, 15 mA max. R = 6.9/0.015 = 460 Ω → use 470 Ω (I = 14.7 mA). A 390 Ω resistor would give 17.7 mA.
