## LESSON gau-L1: Electric potential · Work and potential difference
KEY gau-L1#0 | Electric potential | Potential is energy per charge, V = U/q, measured in volts. A point charge makes V = kq/r with k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². | FORMULA: V = kq/r | CHECK Q: What is the electric potential, in kilovolts, at a distance of 2 m from a point charge of 9 µC? (k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²)
KEY gau-L1#1 | Work and potential difference | Moving a charge q through a potential difference ΔV changes its energy by qΔV. A charge released from rest gains kinetic energy qΔV. | FORMULA: ΔKE = qΔV | CHECK Q: How much energy does a charge of 2 µC gain moving through a potential difference of 270 V? Answer in millijoules.
KEY gau-L1#2 | Uniform fields | Between parallel plates E is uniform and V = Ed. The field points from high to low potential. | FORMULA: V = Ed | CHECK Q: A potential difference of 100 V is across parallel plates 4 cm apart. What is the electric field between them, in V/m?
## LESSON gau-L2: Gauss's law · Field of simple shapes
KEY gau-L2#0 | Gauss's law | The electric flux through a closed surface depends only on the charge inside: Φ = Q_enc/ε₀ with ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²). Symmetry then gives E. | FORMULA: Φ = Q/ε₀ | CHECK Q: A closed surface encloses a net charge of 1 nC. What is the total electric flux through it, in N·m²/C? (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²))
KEY gau-L2#1 | Field of simple shapes | Point charge: E = kq/r². Long line of charge λ: E = λ/(2πε₀r). Infinite sheet σ: E = σ/(2ε₀). Inside a conductor in equilibrium E = 0. | FORMULA: E = kq/r² | CHECK Q: What is the electric potential, in kilovolts, at a distance of 0.2 m from a point charge of 4 µC? (k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²)
KEY gau-L2#2 | Capacitors | Two plates store charge: Q = CV. A parallel-plate capacitor has C = ε₀A/d, and stores energy ½CV². A dielectric multiplies C by κ. | FORMULA: C = ε₀A/d | CHECK Q: A parallel-plate capacitor has plate area 0.1 m² and plate separation 1 mm. What is its capacitance in picofarads?
