# circuit-lab-b / mag: Magnetism & induction  (49 questions, 38 families)
## STUDY NOTE (what students are taught)
Listed on the 2026 Division B page; absent from the 2027 text you were given. Learn the basics in case they return. Poles: like repel, unlike attract. Field lines leave N, enter S, never cross; closer lines = stronger field. Earth’s geographic north is a magnetic south pole. Iron, nickel and cobalt are attracted; copper, aluminum and gold are not. Ørsted: current makes a magnetic field. Straight wire (right-hand rule): thumb along conventional current, fingers curl the way the field circles. Coil: fingers curl with the current, thumb points to the N pole. Electromagnet strength: more turns, more current, soft iron core. It switches off with the current. Motor: electrical → mechanical (force on a current in a field, F = BIL; the commutator reverses current each half turn). Generator: mechanical → electrical. Induction (Faraday): a changing flux through a coil induces a voltage. Faster motion, stronger magnet and more turns give more voltage. Lenz: the induced current opposes the change. Transformer (AC only): V s /V p = N s /N p . Ideal: P in = P out , so stepping voltage down steps current up by the same ratio. Units: tesla (T) for flux density, N/(A·m). Electromagnet station. Wrap many turns of insulated wire around an iron bolt, connect a battery, test with paper clips. More turns or a second cell picks up more. Keep it brief: the wire and battery heat up.
