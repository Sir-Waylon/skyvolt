# meteorology-b / upper: Upper air, soundings, CAPE  (56 questions, 42 families)
## STUDY NOTE (what students are taught)
Standard levels: 850 mb (low moisture), 700 mb (steering), 500 mb (troughs and ridges), 300 mb (jet stream). Hodograph: plots the wind at each height as a point, then connects them. A long, curving path means strong shear. Curving clockwise with height (veering) favors supercells. Stüve / Skew-T: temperature (solid) and dew point (dashed) against pressure. Lines close together = moist or cloudy. Far apart = dry. A layer where temperature rises with height is an inversion (very stable, a "cap"). States/Nationals: LCL (m) ≈ 125 × (T − Td in °C) Dry parcel cools 10 °C per km. Typical air cools about 6.5 °C per km. CAPE (J/kg) Instability Under 1,000 Marginal 1,000–2,500 Moderate 2,500–4,000 Strong Over 4,000 Extreme Low LCL (low cloud base) raises tornado risk. CAPE alone is not enough: you also need shear and a lift trigger.
