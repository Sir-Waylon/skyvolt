# circuit-lab-b / circ: DC circuits  (365 questions, 65 families)
## STUDY NOTE (what students are taught)
Series: same current; voltages add; R = R₁ + R₂ + … Parallel: same voltage; currents add; 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …; two: R₁R₂/(R₁+R₂); n equal: R/n. The result is below the smallest branch. Voltage divider: V₂ = V·R₂/(R₁+R₂). Current divider (two branches): I₁ = I·R₂/(R₁+R₂); the smaller R takes more current. Method for any network Reduce parallel groups and series groups one step at a time until one resistor remains. I_total = V/R_total. Expand back: find the voltage across each group, then the current in each resistor (V = IR). Check: branch currents sum to the node current; drops around the loop sum to the source; Σ resistor power = source power. Worked example. 18 V, R₁ = 6 Ω in series with R₂ = 12 Ω ∥ R₃ = 12 Ω. Parallel pair = 6 Ω; R_total = 12 Ω; I = 1.5 A. Pair voltage = 1.5 × 6 = 9 V; I₂ = I₃ = 0.75 A. Power: 13.5 W + 6.75 W + 6.75 W = 27 W = 18 × 1.5. Brightness and power Series bulbs: same current, so larger R gets more power (P = I²R). Parallel bulbs: same voltage, so smaller R gets more power (P = V²/R). Remove one parallel bulb: the others do not change. Remove one series bulb: all go out. Add a resistor in series: total R rises, current falls. Add one in parallel: total R falls, current rises. Load on a divider: a load across R₂ makes R₂ ∥ R_load, so the output drops. Maximum power transfer (bonus): load power peaks when R_load = R_source; then half the power is lost in the source. Open and short circuits Open: break in the path, I = 0. The break shows the full source voltage across it; every other series part shows 0 V. Short: near-zero resistance path. Current is huge; parts in parallel with the short get ~0 V. A switch that is open has the full battery voltage across its terminals. Meters Voltmeter: in parallel, very high resistance. Ammeter: in series, very low resistance, never across a source. Resistance: power off, isolate the part. Dial: V with a straight line = DC volts; wavy line = AC (never used here). Leads: red in V/Ω, black in COM. Start on the highest range. Current mode acts like a wire; avoid it, or measure V and R and calculate I. Open circuit on an ohmmeter shows OL or blinking digits. Schematic symbols to know Resistor (zigzag or rectangle), battery (long line +, short line −), ground (common 0 V), switch, lamp (circle with X), fuse, LED (diode triangle with two arrows), diode (triangle toward cathode), voltmeter (V in a circle), ammeter (A in a circle). Wires that cross with a dot are connected; without a dot they are not. Trap Parallel formula: 1/R_total is not R_total. Always finish by checking that the answer is smaller than the smallest branch.
