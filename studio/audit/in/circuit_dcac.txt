# circuit-lab-b / dcac: DC & AC  (66 questions, 24 families)
## STUDY NOTE (what students are taught)
DC AC Direction One way Reverses periodically Sources Batteries, solar cells, fuel cells, rectified supplies, USB chargers Alternators and generators, wall outlets Uses Electronics, LEDs, motors on batteries Power distribution (transformers easily change voltage) Typical 1.5, 3, 9, 12 V cells 120 V / 60 Hz (US), 230 V / 50 Hz (many countries) Period T = 1/f. 60 Hz → 16.7 ms; 50 Hz → 20 ms. Why AC transmission uses very high voltage: P = VI, so the same power at higher V needs less current, and line loss is I²R. Conventional current flows from + to − outside the source. Electrons move the other way. Slides use conventional current. Cells: in series the voltages add (4 × 1.5 V = 6 V). In parallel the voltage stays the same and capacity adds. Dry cell = paste electrolyte (alkaline, zinc-carbon); wet cell = liquid electrolyte (lead-acid). A battery is two different metals plus an electrolyte, so a lemon or potato works (about 0.7 V per lemon with copper and steel, about 1.2 V per Cu-Zn potato cell). Hazards: current through the body does the damage. About 1 mA is felt, 10 to 20 mA can lock muscles, about 100 mA across the chest can cause fibrillation. Voltage drives that current through body resistance; wet skin means lower resistance. A short circuit (near-0 Ω path) gives huge current, heat, fire. Never demonstrate one. Rectifier: diodes turn AC into DC (a single diode = half-wave). Household AC (2026 list) US outlet: hot (black, ≈120 V RMS to ground), neutral (white, return path, ≈0 V), ground (green or bare, fault path only). RMS is the DC-equivalent heating value: V rms = V peak /√2, so 120 V RMS ≈ 170 V peak. Breaker panel: 15 A and 20 A branch circuits; GFCI at wet locations. Power in a line is P = VI, so I = P/V.
